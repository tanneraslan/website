Day imagery: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation, July 2004, base map. Reto Stöckli, NASA Earth Observatory.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/

Night lights: NASA Earth Observatory, 2016 Black Marble grayscale global composite. Images by Joshua Stevens, using Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Román, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/

Elevation: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (2022), ETOPO 2022 15 Arc-Second Global Relief Model. Ice surface elevation, global 60 arc-second derivative.
https://doi.org/10.25921/fd45-gt74

Ocean coverage: Natural Earth, 1:50m Ocean, version 5.1.2. Public domain.
https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-physical-vectors/50m-ocean/

The base maps are equirectangular, north up, east right, with the global seam at 180 degrees. Global base day and relief are 1024 x 512. Global base night is 2048 x 1024. The optional 4096 x 2048 detail tiers derive from ETOPO elevation and NASA's 3 km (13500 x 6750) night-light composite. Day refinement comprises six 2048 x 2048 cube faces, or six 1024 x 1024 mobile faces, projected from the 2 km (21600 x 10800) Blue Marble map. Face names follow OpenGL +X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z with no vertical flip. In Earth-fixed axes, +X is the equator at Greenwich, +Y is the equator at 90 E, and +Z is the North Pole. The elevation maps store land height in red and green (0 to 8849 metres, linear) and ocean coverage in blue. Lighting normals are reconstructed in the shader. Day is sRGB; night and relief are sampled as data.

Instanced lamps: Sebastian Lague, Geographical Adventures. Original placements, intensities, and per-lamp dusk variation are packed in city-points.bin. See city-points-license.txt for the source and license.

To regenerate the imagery derivatives, see scripts/bake-earth.py in the source repository.

Cloud coverage: Contains modified EUMETSAT data. Maps processed by Matt Eason. The bundled fallback was retrieved on 2026-11-20.
https://github.com/matteason/live-cloud-maps

Water normals: Tanner Aslan's WebGL engine, resources/normalmap.png (downsampled).
https://github.com/tanneraslan/webgl-engine
Ocean depth: ETOPO 2022 as above. The bathymetry channel encodes sqrt(depth in metres / 11000), clamped to [0,1].
Wind: NOAA GFS, 2016-11-20 00:00 UTC, packed east/north velocity from Mapbox's webgl-wind example. Used for artistic motion, independent of the live cloud map.
https://github.com/mapbox/webgl-wind/tree/master/demo/wind
